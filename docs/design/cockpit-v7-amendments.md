# Cockpit v7: amendments

These override [cockpit-v7.md](cockpit-v7.md). The spec is kept byte for byte as generated (`bun tools/mockgen/gen/build.ts docs/design/cockpit-v7.md`, or `bun run --cwd tools mockgen`, reproduces it), so changes are recorded here and not edited into it. Where an amendment and the spec disagree, the amendment wins.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-21)

### 1. `[ STEPS ]` never lists agents one by one

A parallel launch is one step row: kind `agent`, subject `7 launched`. The roster lives only in `[ AGENTS ]`.

- Replaces: E.12 (the `agent` row of the kind table: subject "the agent's name", result `running` / `done` / `failed`), E.39, and the `[ STEPS ]` section of scenario F.34, which draws seven `agent` rows.
- The row settles once, when the launch call returns. Its result is the count that finished, for example `6 done · 1 failed`, written when the last agent of that launch reports. A failed agent is marked in `[ AGENTS ]`, not in `[ STEPS ]`.
- Several launches in one turn are one row each.

### 2. Every pane section is separated by exactly one blank row

This includes the head: one blank row between `[ NOW ]` / `[ LAST ]` and `[ STEPS ]`, at every pane height.

- Replaces: C.2 ("separated by 1 blank row when the pane has 24 body rows or more, and by none below that") and step 4 of the fitting rule in E.37, whose first move was to drop the blank rows.
- The ladder of E.37 now starts at "`[ SOURCES ]` to its label". A pane too short for the head, one blank row and three step rows shows the head alone.
- `fitPane` in `tools/mockgen/gen/lib.ts` still implements the old rule (`gap: R >= 24 ? 1 : 0`); it is the reference for everything else in E.37, not for this.

### 3. The receipt says `done`, not `done · turn N`

The turn rule already names the turn.

- Replaces: E.1 and E.32 ("the state word and `· turn n` from column 2"), and every receipt in section F.
- Consequence for E.45: the number for `/steps 7` is read from the turn rule `[ TURN 7 ]`, not from the receipt. `p` and `n` in the pane, and a click on a receipt (probe 25 passed), do not need the number at all.

### 4. Fullscreen must be on by default

Cockpit needs the fullscreen layout, and a terminal's layout is fixed for the life of a session (types: `isFullscreen` is "fixed per session").

- The setting is `"tui": "fullscreen"` in `~/.claude/settings.json`. It is already set on this machine (line 136). Nothing was changed.
- Two things turn fullscreen off regardless of that setting, both from the 2.1.329 types: the environment variable `CLAUDE_CODE_NO_FLICKER=0`, and running inside tmux, where the main screen is the default.
- Evidence: a probe session started with `"tui": "fullscreen"` reported `viewport.isFullscreen: true` and `presentation.isFullscreen: true`, and docked the pane. A session with the key absent was not tested, and the key is not described in the plugin types, so "absent means main screen" is unverified.
- Scenario F.40 (toggling fullscreen mid-session) and probe 19 stay unrun: `/tui` writes this setting.

### 5. `/cockpit` docks the pane below the 144-column threshold, down to 110

Confirmed by probe 26: an open the plugin makes on its own is refused below 144 columns; an open made in answer to a command is placed and docked at 120 and at 110; at 109 the engine reseats the pane inline.

- This is what E.24 and C.1 already say. It is recorded here because it is now a requirement and not only a typed fact.

### 6. A failed fetch or search is a source problem, not a turn failure

Owner, 2026-11-22, from the phase 6 screen check: a 404 made the turn `failed` and left `✕ still failing · fetch · httpbin.org/status/404 · 404` above the receipt.

- A `fetch` or `web` step that fails keeps its `✕` and its result in `[ STEPS ]`, and its status in `[ SOURCES ]` (err, bold). It adds no `still failing` row, and it never makes the receipt or `[ LAST ]` say `failed`, nor counts in their failures.
- `still failing` stays for tests, builds, commands, edits and agents: every other kind.
- Replaces: the parts of E.5 and E.32 that count any `✕` step as a failure of the turn.

### 7. The keys the focused pane gets, and the ones the engine keeps

From the phase 7 probe (see the feasibility report). E.44's own keys hold: a single lowercase letter or digit is a `hotkey` Button and fires only while the pane holds the keyboard, never from the prompt. The engine keeps every other key, and none of these reaches Cockpit as a key:

- Tab and Shift+Tab move the focus ring through the pane's Buttons (Cockpit hears `ui.focus`); the arrows, the page keys, Home and End scroll the pane (`ui.scroll`).
- Enter presses the Button under the ring: Cockpit's openable rows are Buttons, so Enter opens the row, but there is no Enter key to bind.
- Escape returns the keyboard to the prompt; Cockpit sees only that the pane is no longer focused. So Escape cannot close a view and leave the pane focused: an unfocused pane is always the fitted layout, and `b` is the way back that keeps the keyboard.
- ctrl+x tab focuses the pane and ctrl+x x closes it.
- Replaces: E.44's "Escape, or `s` again, returns to the fitted layout" (Escape also returns the keyboard), and E.44's step detail `r` key, which is not built: `c` copies a detail's command.

### 8. History keeps every turn to a budget, its bulky parts first to go

Owner, 2026-11-22 (phase 8). Replaces E.45's "the last 50 turns are also written to the plugin's store" and "Output and diffs are not kept".

- Every typed turn is kept, in the session's state and in `$.store` under `history:<session id>`: its steps (subjects cut at 200 characters), files, pages, agents and receipts (the summary rows), and its bulky parts, the steps' output excerpts and patches and each file's diff over the turn.
- The store holds 4 MiB of JSON in all, every session's history together (probe 24). Over the budget, the oldest turn that still has bulky parts loses them first, whichever session it belongs to; with none left, the oldest other session goes; only then this session's oldest summaries. A detail or diff that was trimmed says so.
- A 30-turn session of 20 steps a turn, with excerpts, patches and diffs, is about 1.5 MB, of which about 0.3 MB are summaries.
- A file's diff in `[ FILES ]` is its content at the turn's start (read just before the first call that could change it) against its content now, so a shell's changes are in it. A file no call named before it changed falls back to Git when Git saw it clean at the turn's start; otherwise the view says why it has no fuller diff.
- After a compaction nothing on screen changes: E.45's `· before the compaction` in `[ LAST ]` is not drawn.
- A pin is released by a typed prompt, `/steps` alone, `l`, or pinning the live turn; `p` at the oldest kept turn stays and says `no earlier turn kept`.
- After a resume (`claude --continue`), the transcript the engine draws again ends each answer with its receipt, as the live session did. History messages carry no turn id, so each receipt records the id of its turn's last answer row (the `session.append` uuid, which the engine also draws the row under, live and after a resume), and an answer row finds its receipt, and on a resumed transcript its `claude` label, by that id: two answers with the same text never swap receipts. The engine draws the resumed transcript before `session.start`, so those rows take the left's mode only once the pane is placed. A resumed answer split over several messages gets the label on its last row.
- Each turn rule keeps its submit time after a resume: the transcript keeps the prompts' stamps in `$.store` under `prompts:<session id>`, beside the history. The engine first draws a submitted prompt in a stand-in row (request `placeholder`); that row shows the time without claiming it, so the prompt's own row keeps it.
- A click on a receipt pins its turn and says so in a toast (`pinned turn N · l in the pane or /steps for live`). On the live turn's receipt it pins nothing and says `turn N is the live turn · the pane follows it`. Clicks reach the plugin in the terminal: in fullscreen the engine turns on SGR mouse reporting.
- A click on any Cockpit row (a receipt, a step row, a key hint) only pins, opens a view or says something; it never submits, runs again or rewinds a prompt. Cockpit draws nothing clickable on a prompt row.

### 9. Fallback: what the left shows when no pane is docked

Phase 9, 2026-11-22. Decided while building the feasibility report's phase 9 (capsules, failure rows at once, the full live area, the inline inspector); where E.3, E.5, E.13, E.32, E.33 and E.46 leave a choice open, this is the choice.

- **When it applies.** Cockpit on and its pane not docked: below 110 columns, at 110 to 143 columns without `/cockpit`, outside fullscreen, or after the pane is closed. Each transcript row keeps the mode of its first draw (docked, fallback, or the full view), as phase 4 decided for docked rows. Another pane in the dock hiding Cockpit's tab keeps the full view (phase 5); that is not a fallback case. Cockpit off draws exactly as before.
- **What leaves the left** is what leaves it docked: narration, passing steps, the engine's agent rows, the footer. The engine's "agent finished" notice is also hidden when Cockpit's roster knows the agent, in both modes: the engine's own list drops an agent once it ends, which left the notice drawn.
- **Capsule fields.** The step count is the main loop's (amendment 1: a launch is one step). Agent launches are counted apart as `n agents`, not as a kind, and a failed agent adds `n agents failed` to the story. Subagents' own steps are not counted (out of scope until their calls reach `[ FILES ]` and `[ SOURCES ]`). Kinds give way from the right: three from 72 columns, two below, then one, then none; the clock goes below 60.
- **Trigger 2** (five silent minutes) is decided at the first draw of the next step's site, and the capsule covers the steps settled before it. The engine raises a call's site when the call starts, not when the previous one ends, so this is the first draw that can carry it without changing a row already drawn.
- **Trigger 3** (a turn with no text; replaced by amendment 10, which draws it under the turn's last call): the footer claims, at its first draw, the turn that just ended, and draws that turn's capsule and receipt only when the turn wrote no answer row. A turn that wrote text keeps its receipt under its answer.
- **Failure rows** draw when the step settles, from its kept output, and never change, even once a later step fixes them. A failed fetch or search draws none and is not a capsule failure (amendment 6). `⊘ blocked` rows are not drawn: the step log has no blocked state yet.
- **Vertical rhythm (C.2).** One blank row above the first event row after a prompt or a block of text, none between event rows, and none between an event row and the capsule under it. Decided at each row's first draw.
- **The receipt** carries E.32's `n agents` and `n sources` (pages fetched) in both modes, so the same turn shows the same counts at 200 columns docked and at 100 in fallback. `waiting · n agents` does not repeat its count. Narrow, fields drop in E.32's order (commit, sources, files, agents, steps); exceptions, the state word and the time always stay. In fallback the receipt leaves the steps to the capsule only when the turn drew exactly one. It says them itself whenever no capsule was drawn for its turn (a resumed transcript, a row drawn again after a plugin reload, a turn of no steps, which says `no steps`) or several were: no turn is left without its numbers (E.47). It draws no `still failing` rows (each failure already has its row). Owner, 2026-11-22, from the PR #19 review.
- **The live area** is the `Spinner` site's tree (probe 7: a multi-row tree draws directly above the prompt), not `AbovePrompt`: the now-row (H.3's fallback column), `[ AGENTS ]` while it has agents, and the to-do in progress; at most 9 rows, finished agents collapsing first; across the terminal less 6 columns for the engine's mark. An agent with no steps yet drops the count and its separator (`reading its brief   1s`, not `0 ·  1s`), the time keeping its column, in the live area and in `[ AGENTS ]` alike; this replaces E.40's _Launched_ `0 steps` (owner, PR #19 review). Idle it draws nothing. While a turn runs the engine's `AgentRow` list is hidden (the roster is above it); idle, it shows. `! needs you` is not drawn: no site is raised while a dialog is open (probe 8).
- **`/steps`** with no pane placed opens the pane with the keyboard (`focus`, `closeOnEscape`, `rows: 12`). Below 110 columns or outside fullscreen the engine seats it inline: the inspector, one section `[ STEPS · TURN n ]` (`· PINNED` for a pin), keys `s`, `c`, `p`, `n` (no section keys). Each view's hint row offers exactly the keys that work in it: every step (`s`) offers `s: fit  c: copy  b: back`, one step (Enter) `c: copy  b: back`, both on the box's first row (checked live at 100 columns). From 110 columns in fullscreen an asked open docks (amendment 5), so `/steps` there docks the whole pane, as `/cockpit` does.
- **Not built:** the `│ thought` capsule (Cockpit's log does not keep thinking time), `/todo`, and capsules or failure rows for a resumed transcript or after a plugin reload (each row's decision lives in the module; those rows draw none). Phase 10 keeps the ctrl+o view, resizing across 110 and 144 mid-turn, switching fullscreen, and `/copy`.

### 10. Switching: rows follow the mode in fullscreen, the seat waits for the width, `/tui` says so once

Owner, 2026-11-22: in fullscreen, when the pane leaves or comes back, the rows already on screen are drawn again in the mode the session is now in (E.47 as written). This replaces amendment 9's "each transcript row keeps the mode of its first draw", for that case only. Phase 10 decided the rest as below; evidence is in the feasibility report (probe 19, phase 10).

- **Fullscreen, docked → fallback.** Every row drawn again takes fallback's look: a finished turn gains its capsule, its receipt drops the step count when exactly one capsule was drawn for it (amendment 9's receipt rule), its `✕ still failing` rows give way to the fallback failure row at the step's place, and the running turn's work is in the live area.
- **Fullscreen, fallback → docked.** The capsules and fallback's failure rows go; the docked receipts and their `still failing` rows come back.
- **What a row draws comes from its own data.** A capsule is worked out at every draw from the session's flow (Claude's blocks and calls in stored order, now in `hooks/shared/flow.ts`), the turn's steps (the live log, or history) and each step's start: trigger 1 at a block of text that stays, trigger 2 at a call that starts five minutes or more after the left last drew (a capsule's end, or an event row), trigger 3 in the footer of an engine turn with no answer row. A capsule counts a failure as fixed only by steps up to its own end, so it never changes later. A failure row comes from the settled step and its kept output. So a row draws the same at every width in one mode, after a resume, and after a reload. A turn that wrote no answer row (trigger 3) has its receipt, and in fallback its capsule, under the row of its last call, docked and in fallback: the receipt keeps that call's id (`lastCall`), and receipts are kept in the state and in history's store copy. `TurnDuration` names no turn (phase 5), so the footer draws nothing; this replaces amendment 9's trigger 3, which claimed the turn at the footer's first draw, a memory a reload, a resume and `/tui` lose. A turn that wrote neither text nor a call has no row to carry it, and draws no receipt.
- **Another pane in the dock** in front of Cockpit's tab is still not fallback (amendment 9): in fullscreen, whether a row was drawn beside Cockpit's tab or another pane's is kept from its first draw. Only Cockpit's placed pane with another tab in front counts as hidden; with no pane placed (the main screen, too narrow, closed) a turn's start says nothing about the tab. Nothing is kept for a row while Cockpit is off (a copy of the module that has not read its switch yet).
- **Outside fullscreen** (the main screen, tmux) a row is printed once and cannot be drawn again: it keeps its first draw.
- **The ctrl+o view keeps its own look across a resize** (E.4): quiet, no capsules, steps and sentences as text rows.
- **Thresholds.** A pane nobody asked for leaves the dock below 144 columns: the engine keeps a docked pane down to 110 (probe 18), so Cockpit closes it itself and opens it again unasked, which waits undrawn until 144. A pane asked for with `/cockpit` or `/steps` stays docked down to 110 (amendment 5). Below 110 the engine reseats it inline, and Cockpit closes an inline pane nobody asked for.
- **No flapping.** A close is made at once (the pane leaving covers nothing). Every re-open waits until the width has not changed for 300 ms; a change inside the wait starts it again at the new width. Every open passes `columns` (correction K). Docked, the prompt's hint is measured beside the pane, so the width is taken from the Pane's own render then (live, taking it from the hint re-opened the pane every 0.6 s).
- **Invariants, each with a test** (`tests/cockpit/switching.test.tsx`): no step is lost and no count changes across 200 → 120 → 100 → 150 → 200; the pane never opens on top of the conversation unasked; a row's look depends only on the mode and its own data.
- **`/tui`** (probe 19). The switch remounts the transcript and loads the hooks module again, in the same session, with no new `session.start`; the pane comes back docked with the log of the turns run while it was away. Cockpit says the switch once, as a notice row on the new screen: `fullscreen off · no dock on the main screen · capsules from here · /steps opens the log above the prompt`, and `fullscreen on · cockpit docked at 72 columns · step logs for N turns` (F.40). The `/tui` hook keeps the switch in `$.store` as the command runs, and the first prompt-hint draw of the next copy says it; the next typed turn clears it. A notice row made by the copy that runs `/tui`, or written into its output, does not survive the remount, and the next copy reads `$.state` as it was before the switch's last writes. The env switch `GHOSTFRAME_COCKPIT` is read again by the first draw of each copy (it was read only at `session.start`), and a change redraws.
- **Narration after a resume or a reload.** Live, a text block is narration when a tool call follows it in its turn, read from the flow in `$.state`; a resumed session starts with an empty state, so its blocks were drawn. The flow's text entries now carry their row id (the `session.append` uuid the engine draws them under), each kept turn records its narration's row ids in its history entry (`narration`, in `$.store`, never trimmed), and a block the flow does not hold is narration when its row id is kept as such. In ctrl+o such a sentence draws as a `│` row without its clock.
- **Resizing.** In Windows Terminal the engine draws every 25 to 50 ms while a window edge is dragged, at each width.
  - A pane nobody asked for leaves at its first draw below 144 columns, at once, with no hold: docked (a narrow dock squeezes the transcript), inline, or opened and not drawn yet. It leaves no open waiting behind.
  - A change that keeps the pane docked waits until no draw has brought a new width for 150 ms, then happens once: another body width from 144 columns up, or an asked pane's between 110 and 143. An open is made only for a body the pane does not have and has not asked for (an open's columns stay put while the terminal changes, correction K).
  - An open the engine has not drawn yet is cancelled (below 144) or replaced (another body) by the next draw at a new width, so the engine never places a stale one.
  - An asked pane crossing below 110 is closed at the engine's first inline draw: that one inline frame is the engine's, drawn before any plugin learns the width.
  - With no pane placed, the prompt hint's draw at 144 columns or more opens it once the width holds, at the width rule, but not before the session start's seat has run: the start makes the one open. A pane the person closed stays closed until `/cockpit` or `/steps`.- **A resume's first draws.** The engine draws the resumed transcript before the plugin is loaded (about 1.1 s, its own look: a known limit), then the plugin's hooks draw before `session.start`. Those draws now read the env switch first, take the mode the seat will give (docked from 144 columns in fullscreen), and read the turn's receipts and narration from the store, so the first Cockpit draw is the final one.
- **Replaces:** amendment 9's first-draw rule for fullscreen rows, and amendment 4's "Scenario F.40 (toggling fullscreen mid-session) and probe 19 stay unrun".

### 11. The ctrl+o view and the `/copy` family

Phase 10, 2026-11-22. Where E.4, E.24 and G.78 leave a choice open, this is the choice.

- **Knowing the view.** No site says "this is the ctrl+o view", and a text block's `requestId` is the same in both views. The sites that do say it set a flag: `ToolUse` and `TurnDuration` (`isExpanded`), and `ToolGroup`, which only the folded view draws (`isExpanded: false`). A change redraws every site, so the text blocks follow. Live, the first ctrl+o press drew the full record at once and a second press folded it again.
- **Rows.** A sentence Claude said while working is a `│` row in muted, wrapped, with the turn clock at which the step it announced started. Each step is a row under it: `│`, three cells, the kind (8 cells), the subject, the result word right-aligned at F−10, and the step's own duration (tenths below ten seconds). An edit's diff and a command's or test's output follow in a frame (`┄ [ diff ]`, `┄ [ output ]`); the output is the call's own, in full. The call's `ToolResult` row draws nothing. Narrow, the result word drops below 72 columns and the duration below 60. A launch of several agents is one row (amendment 1). No `∴ thought` rows (feasibility E.6).
- **What stays the engine's** in ctrl+o: the prompt row, each message's time-and-model row, and the turn's `Brewed for` footer.
- **`/steps n all`** prints the same rows for one turn as the command's output (`/steps all` for the live turn); an output there is history's excerpt, and what it left out is said under it. The engine prefixes the first line with `› ghostframe:`.
- **`/copy`** stays the engine's (the last answer); Cockpit answers `code n` and `link n` (the n-th fenced block, without the indent it was nested at, and the n-th link, whole, in the last answer that wrote text), `steps n` (the `/steps n all` rows as plain text, glyphs kept), `turn n` (the nearest prompt the person typed above the turn's answer row, quoted, then the answer) and `diff <path>` (the file's diff over the newest kept turn that changed it, matched by its path or the end of it), each through `$.ui.copy`, each answered with one notice. A wrong n or path says what exists and copies nothing. Any other form, `/copy 2` for one, passes to the engine. The engine labels a plugin's answer to a built-in command `ghostframe:` and drops the arguments from the echoed command row.

### 12. The turn rule: `─ [ TURN n ] ─── 09:23`

2026-11-22, a follow-up to phase 10. E.1's opening rule was never built: the transcript still drew v6's `+┄┄┄┄ 09:23 ┄┄┄+`, so after amendment 3 no row on the left named a turn's number. Where E.1, E.22, E.23 and E.29 leave a choice open, this is the choice.

- **Where it draws.** While Cockpit is on, every row Cockpit draws (docked, fallback and the ctrl+o view) opens a typed prompt (`UserMessage`, origin `composer`) with the rule. In ctrl+o the prompt row stays the engine's (amendment 11) and the rule sits above it. A row in the full view (Cockpit off, or another pane in front of Cockpit's tab, amendment 9) keeps v6's rule. Agent reports and notices are not typed and get no rule. A Remote Control prompt (origin `bridge`) is a typed turn and takes a number, but its row stays the engine's: no rule.
- **Anatomy.** `─` at column 0, `[ TURN n ]` from column 2, a solid `─` fill and the 24-hour clock ending on the receipt's right edge. Its width is the receipt's (`ruleWidth`, else the layout's width): 108 cells beside a 72-column pane at 200 columns (a 127-column region, correction A), 98 at 100 columns, the same in ctrl+o at 200. E.1's paint: `─` and the fill in rule, the brackets dim, `TURN n` bright, the state and the clock muted. A row redrawn at a new width takes that width (amendment 10).
- **Narrow.** Below 40 cells, or when the clock would leave no fill, the clock goes. The label never does: a row narrower than the label draws the label alone and the terminal cuts it.
- **One number.** n is the step log's `turnNo`, the number `[ NOW ]`, `[ LAST ]`, `/steps n`, `/steps n all`, `/copy steps n` and `/copy turn n` use. Each typed prompt's stamp (`prompts:<session id>` in `$.store`, amendment 8) now also keeps the row the engine draws it under (the `session.append` uuid of its prompt row: live, the submit lands first and the row's `requestId` is that uuid), its number and its label state. A number is claimed only by a turn that starts: the stamp takes it from the log at `turn.start`, never at the submit. The engine may draw the row before the turn starts, so a rule whose stamp has no number yet reads the log at draw time: the log's number when the log's turn started from that stamp, one more than it while that stamp's turn is about to start, else none. A guess is never stored, so a turn that never starts leaves no number behind. A rule finds its stamp by its row, so two prompts with the same text never swap numbers; a stamp kept before this amendment, with no row, is claimed by text as before.
- **The stand-in row** (request `placeholder`) reads the stamp and claims nothing. The number lives in the stamp, not in a claim, so it is never taken twice.
- **After a resume or a reload.** The engine draws the resumed transcript before `session.start`; with the state still empty, the rule reads the stamps from the store, so its first Cockpit draw shows the number and time it had live. Nothing comes from a first draw's memory.
- **Label states.** One state, from what came right before the turn's start. `· RESUMED`: the session's start took the stamps back from the store into an empty state (a restart; a reload keeps its state and is not a resume). `· AFTER COMPACT`: a `session.compact` of the main conversation that installed a summary (trigger `manual`, `auto` or `plugin`; not `precompute`, which installs nothing, nor a skipped one). The latest of the two wins. `· AFTER REWIND`: with neither, when `$.session.messages({ after })` denies the previous typed prompt's row (a row cleared, rewound or left behind a compaction).
- **`AFTER COMPACT` and amendment 8.** Amendment 8 says nothing on screen changes after a compaction: it is about what is already drawn, the pane and `[ LAST ]`, which do not change. The label is on a rule drawn after the compaction, so it changes nothing already on screen. No conflict; it is built.
- **A rewind does not renumber.** E.29 draws `[ TURN 13 · AFTER REWIND ]` after a rewind to turn 12. Here the next turn takes the next number (turn 6 after turn 5 was undone, live): the undone turns keep their step logs and numbers (E.29: they can still be pinned), so reusing a number would make `/steps n` name two turns. Live, the engine removes the rewound turn's rows from the screen.
- **The day.** When the calendar day (local) differs from the previous typed prompt's, the clock reads `Sun 22 Nov · 09:02`.
- **A prompt with no number** (typed while Cockpit was off) draws `[ TURN ]` with its clock: no step log has its turn.
- **v6's rule stays.** `Rule()` in `hooks/shared/rule.tsx` still draws Cockpit-off prompts and every Cockpit-off footer, so it is not deleted. Cockpit off draws exactly as before; the new stamp fields are written only while Cockpit is on. The state's shape tag stays `prompts-1`: the fields are optional, and a new tag would empty the state on an upgrade, which the next session start would read as a resume.
- **Prompts typed while a turn runs (E.20), what the engine does.** Live in 2.1.330, the engine takes such a prompt one of two ways, and the `chat:queueSubmit` key (`wait`) does not choose between them (its types say it queues every mid-turn prompt either way). Both start with `prompt.submit` carrying the running turn's `turnId`, then a `session.append` with door `queue`, while the engine draws the prompt under short-lived rows of its own.
  - _Folded in._ At the running turn's next tool boundary the engine delivers it into that turn: a `session.append` with door `delivery`, an attachment row whose text frames the prompt (`The user sent a new message while you were working:`), whose uuid is the row it draws the prompt under from then on, a resume included. No `turn.start` follows. Seen: ten `sleep 2 && echo sN` calls, a prompt sent during the fourth, delivered after it.
  - _A turn of its own._ When the running turn ends with no tool boundary left (a long answer with no tools), the prompt waits. After `turn.complete` the engine stores it as typed (door `prompt`, the stand-in row first) and starts its turn (`turn.start` with the prompt's text), with no second submit.
- **What the rule does with them.** A prompt typed over a running turn keeps that turn's id on its stamp (`over`). Delivered, its stamp takes the delivery's row and the turn clock it arrived at (`steerMs`): a steer, drawn as E.20 says, a prompt row with no turn rule, `steer` and the turn clock on its time edge (`steer · 14s`), docked, in fallback and after a resume; in ctrl+o the engine's row with no rule. While it waits it draws no rule and nothing on the edge. Started as its own turn, it takes the next number at that `turn.start`, found by its text among the stamps over a turn that has ended. An agent's report starting a turn meanwhile takes neither the stamp nor a number. Before this, the submit predicted a number for every prompt, so a folded one kept a number no turn had: live, the next typed turn showed the same number again.
- **Agents after a resume (amendments 8, 9).** The engine's agent list is empty after a resume and the restored roster is the last turn's, so an earlier turn's `› Message from @Explore` and `◇ Agent … finished` rows showed. Whether a row is one of this session's agents now reads the live roster and every kept turn's roster, from the store for rows drawn before `session.start`. A peer's message names its sender by the agent's name or type (`Explore`), not the roster's description, so a roster entry also keeps those (`called`). A failed agent's fallback `✕` row reads the same kept turns.
- **The store.** The budget weighed `prompts:<id>` as fixed, so a session's stamps outlived its history, and a Cockpit-off session's stamps were never evicted. Each session's stamps now weigh with its history and are deleted with it. Stamps with no history are a session of their own, the oldest evicted first. This session's own stamps are weighed as the store held them at the start (at most 100 stamps of 200 characters). A stamp keeps the first 200 characters of its prompt, so a pasted log costs the store nothing. Its row finds it by id, and claims by text compare the first 200 characters. Turn numbers come from the records' and the stamps' own `turnNo`, never from positions: with this session's oldest summaries trimmed away, the rules, `[ LAST ]`, `/steps n` and `/steps n all` still agree. A log restored from a trimmed or lost history carries on from the highest number the stamps hold, so no number is used twice.
- **`/steps n all` after a resume.** It printed `no step log for turn n` for every turn from before the resume while listing it as kept: its rows were placed by the session's flow, which a resume does not bring back. With no flow left for the turn, its kept steps now print in order, each with its kept output or diff, without the sentences. A kept turn with no steps says `turn n had no steps` (in `/steps n all` and `/copy steps n`); `no step log for turn n · kept: …` is only for a turn Cockpit does not keep.
- **Resumed answers while the first new turn runs.** Live in Windows Terminal, after `--continue` and while the first new turn started, the resumed transcript showed only prompts and rules: every answer, receipt and the story drew as nothing until that turn ended. The cause: while a turn runs, a text block the flow does not hold is taken as the running turn's text not yet known not to be narration, and waits unseen (amendment 10's rule against flashes). The flow lives in the state, which a new process starts empty, so every resumed block looked like the running turn's. The same live placement also put each resumed answer at the running turn's end, so it lost `claude`. And in the moment between Cockpit marking the session started and history restoring the receipts, a draw found no receipts, so its first draw after the plugin loaded had no `claude` either. Now a row first drawn before `session.start` with no turn running (the engine draws the resumed transcript first, amendment 8) is a start row, never the running turn's: never hidden as undecided, never placed in that turn. And receipts read from the store whenever the state holds none. Live, `$.session.messages()` gives no uuids, so the rows cannot be read from it. A resumed answer now draws as it stays, `claude` and its receipt included, on its first draw and while the new turn runs; kept narration still leaves the transcript (amendment 10).
- **A read's line count.** A `Read` step's result counts the lines of the content the read returned, so the same file reads the same: a trailing newline ends the last line and adds none (`hello\r\n` is `1 line`, as `wc -l` says), and a blank line before it counts. The engine's `numLines` is used only when the result has no content; it had said `2 lines` for `hello\r\n` in one turn and 1 in another. One line is `1 line`, never `1 lines`.
- **Evidence.** Live in the ConPTY driver, Claude Code 2.1.330, fullscreen: at 200 columns three turns (one launching an Explore agent, whose report came as a turn of its own) drew `TURN 1` to `TURN 3`, the report no rule, `[ LAST ] … turn 3`, `/steps 2 all` turn 2's launch; after `/exit` and `--continue` the rules redrew with their times and the next turn was `[ TURN 4 · RESUMED ]`. The same at 100 columns in fallback, then `/compact` and a turn (`[ TURN 5 · AFTER COMPACT ]`), then `/rewind` to before it and the prompt again (`[ TURN 6 · AFTER REWIND ]`). For mid-turn prompts, at 200 columns: `TURN 3` ran the ten sleeps, the prompt sent during them drew `steer · 14s` with no rule, the tool-less story was `TURN 4`, and the prompt sent during it became `TURN 5`. After `/exit` and `--continue`, turns 1 to 5 redrew with their numbers, the steer kept its edge, no agent row showed, and the next turn was `[ TURN 6 · RESUMED ]`, with `[ LAST ] turn 6`. At 100 columns in fallback: an agent turn, then the steer (`steer · 16s`); after the resume no agent row, and `[ TURN 3 · RESUMED ]`. Tests: `tests/transcript/turn-rule.test.ts`, `tests/transcript/ledger/stamps.test.ts`, `tests/transcript/sites/prompt.rule.test.tsx`, `tests/plugin/turn-numbers.test.tsx`.

### 13. Agents' own work, an agent's conversation, and openable sources

Phase 11, 2026-11-23. Where E.13, E.40, E.42, E.43 and E.44 leave a choice open, this is the choice. Evidence is in the feasibility report (phase 11 probes and live checks).

- **An agent's own steps** (`RosterAgent.log`, `logDropped`): each subagent's calls are rows of its own log, kept on its roster entry, written by the main loop's own step functions (kind, subject, result, mark, settled once), on the agent's own clock (ms since its launch). A nested agent's launch is a row of its parent's log and settles when the child reports (amendment 1). The engine's report call (`SubagentHandback`) and its plumbing (`ToolSearch`) draw no row and are not counted. At most 500 rows an agent; older ones are counted, not kept. Only agents the roster lists count: a workflow's agents and the engine's own forks (compaction, memory) carry ids no list names, and their calls add no step, file, page or detail. An agent's steps never enter the main loop's log: `[ STEPS ]`, the capsule, `still failing` rows and the step counts stay the main loop's (amendment 9).
- **An agent's details** (output excerpt, patch) are kept beside the main loop's, so Enter on one of its steps opens its detail, labelled `STEP n · AGENT name`. The newest 300 of the main loop's details and the newest 300 of agents' are kept apart, so agents never push the main loop's out. In history its steps are summary rows (subjects cut at 200) and its details bulky parts, trimmed with its turn's (amendment 8); a trimmed turn also keeps only each agent's newest 20 steps, the rest counted, since a background agent alive across turns is saved with each of them.
- **`[ FILES ]` and `[ SOURCES ]` hold agents' work.** An agent's edits and writes, its shell's changes (the Git movement since the last diff) and deletions, and its fetches and searches join the turn's lists. A row an agent touched carries `◇` in dim at column 0, set once and never taken back; a file changed by both the main loop and an agent is one row, its lines summed. An agent's call reads a file before it can change it, as a main-loop call does, so a file's diff stays its content at the turn's start against now; two calls naming the same file at once share one read. An agent's to-do calls do not touch the main list. Calls are noted into the lists one at a time, in the order they end, so two shells ending together never count one change twice. Known limit: a shell step is credited with every change since the last diff, so an agent's shell beside the main loop's may take the other's `◇`; the counts stay right.
- **Counts (E.32, amendment 9).** The receipt's `n files` and `n sources` count agents' files and pages too, once each beside the main loop's, in the first receipt written after they settle (a background agent's work lands between engine turns: its report turn's receipt counts it); a receipt clears only what it counted. Docked and fallback read the same receipt. Step counts stay the main loop's.
- **Failures (amendments 1 and 6).** An agent's failed fetch or search keeps its `✕` and status in its own log and in `[ SOURCES ]`, and is never a turn failure. An agent's failed edit or command that it does not fix is counted on its roster row, `✕n` in err before its step count; it is never a main-loop failure row.
- **Opening an agent (E.40).** Each `[ AGENTS ]` row is a Button. Enter calls `$.ui.view({ agentId })` while it answers the press; `[ STEPS ]` then lists that agent's own steps as `[ STEPS · AGENT name ]` (text-bright), with `agent in view · m for main` under the label. While an agent is in view, every view of the focused pane offers the hotkey `m` (`m: main` in the hint row), which calls `$.ui.view({})` as the answer to the person's own key, from any row: the ring may sit on one of the agent's step rows, where Enter opens that step. Enter on the agent's own row also calls `$.ui.view({})`. The agent runs on; only the view changes. The pane reads which agent is in view from its `view` prop at every draw, so the label follows a switch the person makes from the engine's own tasks list too.
- **Escape is not offered** to leave an agent's view (replaces E.40's "or Escape at the prompt"): while an agent is in view the hint row drops `esc: prompt` (PR #22 live review: with the ring on an agent's step row, Enter opened the step and Escape then stopped the running agent). Live, Escape at the prompt in a viewed agent's conversation first stopped that agent's running background shell (the engine's hint read `esc to stop`); it went back to the main conversation only when nothing of the agent's was running. A new main turn starting also put the main conversation back in view.
- **A view the engine refuses.** The engine drops a finished agent from its list after a while (seen from about a minute to two after its end) and holds none after a resume; `$.ui.view` then answers `no agent under that id`. The pane still lists the agent's kept steps under `[ STEPS · AGENT name ]`, with `conversation not held · <the engine's reason> · b: back`, and offers `b`. Enter on that row again, `b`, or the pane losing the keyboard, goes back.
- **Sources (E.43).** No call in the types opens a URL, and a `Link` opens only on a click, the terminal's (probe 3). So each `[ SOURCES ]` row is a Button: Enter copies the page's URL and says `url copied · no key opens a browser`, in the fitted pane and in the `u` view; `c` on the row is the same call and says the same. A click does the same, and never submits anything (amendment 8). `u` shows every page with its whole URL on the rows under it, plain text in dim from column 4, cut into the pane's width by grapheme. The URL is not drawn as a `Link`: where the engine judges the terminal has no OSC 8, a Link draws its text and then the URL again, which would print it twice (hooks/transcript/draw/parts.tsx).
- **After a resume** an agent's kept steps, files, pages and marks come back from the store with its turn's roster, as the main loop's do (amendment 8); Enter on it lists its kept steps (the engine holds no agent then).
- **Not built:** opening a source in the browser from a key (no API); Escape to leave an agent's view (above).
